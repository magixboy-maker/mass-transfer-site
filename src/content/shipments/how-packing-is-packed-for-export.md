# How Tower Packing Is Packed and Loaded for Export

Most buyers see packing only twice: in a photo before loading, and when the container is opened at site. What happens in between decides whether the material arrives in the condition it was inspected in. This is the standard sequence we follow, written so you can check it against your own receiving requirements.

## 1. Bagging and unit weights

Ceramic and plastic random packing is normally supplied in woven bags, with the unit weight agreed at order stage so it matches the lifting equipment at your site. Metal random packing goes into bags or cartons depending on size — small nominal sizes are easier to handle boxed, larger rings bagged.

If your site uses jumbo bags with a discharge spout, say so at order stage. Re-bagging after the fact costs more than planning for it.

## 2. Structured packing

Structured packing is the fragile item in any shipment. Blocks are kept in their original orientation, banded, and packed in crates or on dedicated frames so that the sheets are not loaded in compression. Crates are lined so that steel banding does not bear directly on the packing.

Never stack crates of structured packing on their side, and do not use them as a base for other cargo when unloading.

## 3. Crating and palletising

Wooden crates are used where the material must survive multiple handling steps, or where the destination requires fumigated timber. We confirm ISPM 15 treatment and marking requirements before packing, because a container held at destination for a missing stamp costs far more than the crate.

Pallets are wrapped and banded, with corner protection so the banding does not cut into the outer layer.

## 4. Moisture protection

Sea freight means condensation. Ceramic packing is unaffected by water but its packaging is not; metal packing can show surface staining after a long voyage in a closed container. Desiccant and a vapour barrier are used where the material or the destination calls for it, and containers are checked for roof condition before loading.

## 5. Loading

Loading is planned so that heavy crates sit low and against the container structure, with the load blocked against movement in all three directions. Packing that can shift in transit arrives with damaged edges — the material is often still usable, but the claim discussion is not worth it.

Where a shipment fills part of a container, the remaining space is braced rather than left to be filled with whatever is available at the port.

## 6. Documents and marking

Shipping marks, packing lists and the material certificates travel with the shipment. Marking is agreed before packing, not painted on afterwards. If your receiving department needs specific label content — item code, heat number, pallet count — send the template with the order.

## What to send us with an enquiry

- Unit weight and package type you can handle at site
- Whether timber must be ISPM 15 treated and how it should be marked
- Any restriction on desiccant or barrier film at destination
- Label template, if you have one

Send these with the technical enquiry and the packing is settled before production rather than after it.
