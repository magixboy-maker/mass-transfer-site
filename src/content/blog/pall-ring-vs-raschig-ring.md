# Pall Ring vs Raschig Ring: Which Random Packing to Choose

When specifying random packing for a distillation or absorption column, the choice often comes down to the pall ring versus the Raschig ring. Both are hollow-cylinder geometries, but they perform very differently — and choosing the wrong one costs you either capacity or money.

## The Basic Difference

A **Raschig ring** is a simple hollow cylinder whose height equals its diameter. There is nothing inside it. It was the first widely used random packing and remains popular for simple or fouling service.

A **pall ring** is a Raschig cylinder that has been modified with internal ribs and open windows cut into the wall. These changes increase the available surface area and improve liquid and gas distribution through the bed.

That single design difference produces large performance gaps.

## Capacity and Pressure Drop

Pall rings typically deliver **roughly 40% higher capacity** than an equivalent Raschig ring, at a similar or lower pressure drop. The open windows let gas and liquid move more freely through the bed, reducing resistance while increasing contact.

For a given throughput, a pall ring therefore allows a **smaller column diameter** — and a smaller column means lower vessel cost and less structural support.

## Efficiency

The internal ribs of a pall ring break up the liquid film and increase the wetted surface, which improves mass transfer. In most duties a pall ring achieves a lower HETP (height equivalent to a theoretical plate) than a Raschig ring of the same nominal size.

The exception is at very low liquid loads, where the simpler Raschig geometry can wet more predictably.

## Cost

Raschig rings are the cheapest packing to make, because they are a simple formed cylinder with no secondary stamping. Pall rings cost more per unit volume because of the extra forming steps.

But the **total installed cost** is what matters. A pall ring's higher capacity often pays for the premium many times over through a smaller column, less packing volume, and lower operating energy.

## When to Choose Each

**Choose a Raschig ring when:**

- The duty is very simple and capacity is not the constraint
- The stream is dirty or fouling and you want maximum robustness
- You are replacing like-for-like in an existing column
- You need the lowest possible material cost

**Choose a pall ring when:**

- You want to maximise capacity per column diameter
- Pressure drop matters (for example, vacuum service)
- You are sizing a new column and want the best efficiency per volume
- Corrosion resistance is needed and you want a modern, proven geometry

## The Bottom Line

For most new columns, the pall ring is the better engineering choice: more capacity, lower pressure drop and better efficiency, at a modest cost premium that is usually recovered through the smaller column it enables. The Raschig ring still earns its place in simple, dirty or very cost-sensitive service.
