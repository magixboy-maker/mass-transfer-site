# Wire Mesh vs Vane Mist Eliminator: How to Choose

Two mist eliminator technologies dominate industrial gas–liquid separation: the wire mesh pad and the vane (chevron) separator. They suit very different conditions, and choosing the wrong one leads to plugging, carryover or excessive pressure drop.

## Wire Mesh Pads

Wire mesh demister pads are made of knitted wire mesh, layered and compressed to a target density. As gas passes through the fine mesh, droplets impinge on the wires, coalesce and drain.

**Strengths:**

- Very high efficiency — over 99% removal of droplets above 3–5 µm
- Extremely low pressure drop, typically 25–250 Pa
- Low cost and simple installation

**Weaknesses:**

- Limited to low gas velocities
- Poor tolerance of high liquid load
- Plugs easily in fouling or solids-laden service

Wire mesh is the default choice for clean streams at moderate velocity — evaporators, knock-out drums, compressor suction and absorber overheads.

## Vane (Chevron) Separators

Vane mist eliminators use closely spaced profiled blades that force the gas through sharp direction changes. Droplets cannot follow the turns and are thrown onto the blade surfaces, where they drain.

**Strengths:**

- Handle far higher gas velocities than mesh
- Handle high liquid loads without re-entrainment
- Tolerate fouling and solids, and can often be washed in place

**Weaknesses:**

- Higher pressure drop than mesh
- Less efficient for sub-10 µm droplets

Vane separators are the choice for FGD scrubbers, cooling towers, gas dehydration and any high-velocity or dirty stream.

## How to Decide


| Condition | Recommendation |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Clean gas, moderate velocity, fine droplets | Wire mesh pad |
| High gas velocity | Vane separator |
| High liquid load | Vane separator |
| Fouling or solids present | Vane separator |
| Need to wash in place | Vane separator |
| Lowest pressure drop | Wire mesh pad |
| Sub-10 µm droplet removal | Wire mesh (or mesh over vane) |


## Combining Both

Where you need both fine-droplet removal and high capacity, a two-stage arrangement — a vane separator for bulk removal followed by a wire mesh polishing stage — is a proven solution.
